### How to display the projects on your website

Start with a compact grid of four project cards. Each card should contain:

- A screenshot or product mockup.
- A generic project title.
- A two-line description.
- Three to five primary technologies.
- A “View Case Study” link.

The link should open a dedicated project page or a detailed modal with the problem, solution, architecture, implementation details, and outcomes.

Don't put the full case study on the home page. Let visitors scan the projects first, then choose what they want to explore.

Also, use your own screenshots wherever possible. Generated visuals can make a layout look polished, but they are not evidence of what you actually built.

## 14. My final recommendation

Prioritize these three things:

1. Make the hero and About sections compelling. Establish that you're a Full Stack Developer with strong frontend skills, real backend experience, and practical AI integration experience.
2. Make the projects your strongest evidence. The case studies should show engineering decisions, not just technology names. Your data-heavy timeline, voice/text form builder, and AI automation work provide particularly useful examples.
3. Keep everything credible. Four well-explained projects with accurate outcomes are more impressive than ten shallow project cards with inflated claims.

One final point: you do not need to include every technology, every responsibility, or every detail from both resumes. Your portfolio should tell a coherent story about the engineer you are, backed by specific work and outcomes.

The positioning I would use consistently throughout the site is:

> I build scalable, user-focused web applications by combining strong frontend engineering, reliable backend systems, and practical AI-powered capabilities.

That gives you room to apply for Frontend Engineer, React Developer, Full Stack Developer, and Product Engineer opportunities without making the portfolio feel unfocused.
